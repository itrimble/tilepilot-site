Features

Built around how hands actually drag.

Every trigger in TilePilot was tuned with real end-to-end input tests — not just unit math.

Snapping

Quadrant regions

Drop a window anywhere decisively inside a screen quadrant and it becomes that quarter. No hunting for a 20-pixel edge sliver while your window hangs off-screen.

Halves & maximize

Edge middles mean halves; the top edge maximizes. Corner approaches from any direction give quarters, with generous radial corner targets.

Free placement stays free

The center cross of each display never snaps, and holding ⌘ while dragging suppresses snapping entirely. Clicks and text-selection drags are verified as non-moves and never snap.

Multi-monitor that behaves

Drags stay on the display you're working on through seam overshoot; crossing to another display takes a deliberate move. Fast flicks deep into a neighbor transfer instantly.

Keyboard snapping

Halves, quarters, thirds, center, maximize, restore, and display moves — every command on a rebindable shortcut.

Gaps & corner fitting

Optional gap between snapped windows, and corner-fit logic that adjusts neighbors when a window needs more than one quadrant.

Workspaces

Capture

One click snapshots your running apps, their window frames, and the display arrangement they belong to — with live preview thumbnails.

Restore

Bring the whole layout back on demand: apps relaunch when needed and windows return to their captured frames on the right displays.

Display-aware

Workspaces remember which monitor arrangement they were captured on — built for laptop-dock life where displays come and go.

Switcher presets

Coming from Windows

⌘-arrow left/right snapping, maximize, restore, and quarter cycling — the muscle memory you already have.

Keep Mac feel

Rectangle-style ⌃⌥ shortcuts for halves, quarters, thirds, center, and display moves.

Linux Power User

Modal resize and move shortcuts today; Vim-style directional focus is on the roadmap.
